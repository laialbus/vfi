Two filers now have their facts in the tree, and one of them has a kind. M4-21
asserted the first, for the reason that its statements were the only ones to
argue from; M4-20 landed the second, CIK 0001859199, recorded for the
fiscal-year change the milestone names among its golden fixtures. This is the
same task for the second filer, and it is queued on its own rather than left to
the fixture that will be written against it, because the two are decided
against different evidence: a kind is read off the filer's balance sheet and
income statement against the vocabulary's clause, and a fixture's expected
result is read off the ruleset.

The fixture needs the kind settled first. Rule 1 of the accepted
`docs/adr/period-alignment.md` makes a filer's period set a function of it — "a
period is the filer's when at least one concept its kind admits resolves to a
`Value` at it" — so an expected result written for a filer with no kind would be
written against a set that changes the day one is asserted. And a second real
filer with a kind is a second filer that can reach `NotApplicable`, which is one
of the three states the milestone requires and which no test may reach by
naming a kind for itself.

This filer is harder to read than the first, and the acceptance is stricter for
it. Its element names come from more than one shape — a classified balance sheet
and a revenue-to-cost-of-revenue run beside real-estate, equity-method and
held-in-trust elements — and its history crosses a change of calendar, which the
vocabulary treats as a change nothing about a kind may straddle: "a concept not
applicable in one period and a value in the next means the kind changed." So the
argument reads both sides of the change, refuses each other kind by a fact
rather than by silence, as the first file does, and asserts nothing where the
facts disagree. An unassigned filer costs precision and never produces a wrong
answer; a kind asserted over a pivot produces exactly the wrong answer this
milestone exists to prevent.

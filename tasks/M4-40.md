The second of the two open questions the 2026-09-18 planner entry names:
"whether re-recording the benchmark baseline for a stage that now does work
counts as moving the gate". It stands in the same place as M4-39 — between the
wiring and the six fixtures — because the fixtures cannot run over the stage
until the stage's entry point is the stage, and the moment it is, the committed
baseline measures something that is gone.

It is above a worker for the plainest reason there is: the run that would
re-record the baseline is the run whose own change the baseline exists to
judge. Nothing in the tree says today whether that is maintenance or a gate
moved out of the way, and an agent deciding it inside its own boundary decides
it for every stage that comes after.

Independent of M4-39, which asks what writes a fixture's expected result. The
two are the same milestone's obstacle and neither reads the other's answer, so
they own different files and are not ordered.

Not in this diff: the new workload, the new baseline, and the change to the
stage's entry point that forces both. The record is a proposal; the decider
reviews it, and the work it hands on is queued after it is accepted.

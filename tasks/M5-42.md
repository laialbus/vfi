GOALS.md's fifth M5 criterion names the dividend growth rate beside the
streak. `docs/adr/which-duration-precedes-a-duration.md`, accepted and
built by M5-36, fixes everything around the rate: the window as the
setting `dividend_growth_years`, the series it reads, the period it is
stated at, and the absences. It leaves the formula and the tolerance of a
one-period excess open by name. The streak was computed under the same
record by M5-37; the rate was not, because its formula is unfixed.

Why a record, and not the metric's task. Two planning runs held the rate
rather than scope it, because no open source for its form was found and a
task names only a source its planner opened. The form is a method choice
between readings the sources make differently: a compound rate over the
endpoints, which the arithmetic record anticipates by its n-th root, reads
nothing between them and so is moved by an excess only at an endpoint; a
mean of changes reads every year and is moved by an excess twice, up then
down. Which one the derivation takes, and what it does at a special
dividend, is the exposure the open-questions record recorded as landing on
M5, and the dividend discount valuation will read the answer again. One
record settles it with its reason, as `common-shareholder-forms.md` and
`capital-employed-invested-capital-and-nopat.md` settled their terms.

It depends on nothing because everything it reads is on main: the series
record and M5-36's names, the arithmetic record, the analyze record, the
open-questions record and the sources record. It owns one new file, so it
runs beside M5-40 and M5-41, which own `crates/analyze/`. The metric is
queued under the record's acceptance, not here.

If the record finds the formula cannot be stated without an operation
outside the arithmetic record's table, a seventh reason, a change to a
frozen contract's bytes, or an edit to the series record, it says so by
name as the acceptance above provides. If it cannot be written at all
without an accepted record changing, that is an escalation.

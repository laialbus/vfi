GOALS.md's fourth M5 criterion ends with "discounted cash flow with margin
of safety", and its ninth says a metric with no price data "never shows a
wrong number". `docs/adr/what-analyze-takes-and-returns.md` (Accepted)
fixes the share-basis rule that keeps a price off a figure on another
share basis, in three clauses that compare dates, and asks that it be
written once as "the one function every price-dependent metric calls".
`docs/adr/discounted-cash-flow-and-margin-of-safety.md`, accepted on
2026-10-19, makes the margin of safety the first metric to read a price, so
the first to write that rule, and names one question it cannot answer:
whether the rule may compare dates through the one ordering
`docs/adr/which-duration-precedes-a-duration.md` (Accepted) added to
`date.rs`, which that record says "is used by the alone clause and by
nothing else" and "never picks the latest". The covered range the rule reads
is the earliest and latest trading date among the prices, which is picking
the latest. Read as written, the sentence stops the first task to write the
rule, and the discounted cash flow record says so.

Why a record, and not the margin's task. A worker that meets a sentence of
an accepted record forbidding what its task needs stops and escalates; the
accepted record says as much. The decider flagged the question for human
review beside the tier when it accepted the record. One record settles it
with its reason, as `sources-a-task-can-read.md` settled one sentence of
the period record, and the margin's task is queued under its acceptance.

It depends on nothing because everything it reads is on main. It owns one
new file, so it runs beside M5-42, which owns a record file of another
name, and beside the three tasks on `crates/analyze/`.

If the record finds the rule cannot be written within the six reasons, or
cannot be stated without a frozen contract's bytes changing, it says so by
name as the acceptance above provides. If it cannot be written at all
without an anchor changing, that is an escalation.

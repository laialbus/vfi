acceptance:
  - The seed set is read from what EDGAR publishes, not from a list committed
    here. A seed entry that resolves to no identifier is itself a verdict at the
    seed step, keyed by whatever there was.
  - The metadata gate judges a filer from its metadata alone, without retrieving
    that filer's history. A step that costs what the step after it costs is not a
    gate.
  - Every rule the gate applies is named, defined once beside the step that
    issues it, and traces to a line in GOALS.md. The exclusion GOALS states
    outright is foreign private issuers filing 20-F. A rule GOALS does not
    support is an escalation, not a rule invented to fit — what this gate rejects
    is what the tool will never see again.
  - Every filer the gate evaluates has a verdict recorded through M3-05's
    interface, the rejected ones included, each carrying its named reason and the
    values it was judged on.
  - A filer the gate could not judge — metadata that did not arrive, or that does
    not say — is recorded as not judged. It is never recorded as rejected.
  - The admitted set is derived from the verdicts recorded, not assembled beside
    them. A filer that reaches the next step without a record should be
    impossible by construction, not caught by a reviewer.
  - A filer the gate rejects costs nothing further: no request is sent for it
    after its verdict is recorded.
  - The behaviour is pinned by golden fixtures under `fixtures/fetch/`, recorded
    from live EDGAR and committed as data, covering an admitted filer, a rejected
    one, and one that could not be judged.
  - Every request leaves through M3-01's chokepoint; nothing added here opens a
    connection around it.
  - No test reaches the live network, and nothing proved gets narrower: every
    existing gate, proof, and fixture case still passes.
---

This is the first two thirds of M3's funnel criterion and the whole of its
verdict criterion for the filers that never reach a history request — which is
most of them, and the ones the ledger exists for. A rejected filer produces no
filings, no metrics, and no row a user can ask about; the verdict is the only
trace it was ever considered.

The rules are the part to be careful about. GOALS.md holds the premises about
what VFI is for and what it is not: US filings from EDGAR, value and dividend
analysis over a corpus, and a scope note that excludes foreign private issuers
filing 20-F. Derive the gate from those and cite each rule to the line it comes
from, the way a methodology constant cites its paper. Do not add a rule because
it seems prudent — a filer wrongly rejected here is invisible downstream, and
"looked reasonable" is not a reason anyone can check in December.

Record the fixtures from EDGAR rather than writing them from memory, as M3-03
did: a hand-written response in nearly the right shape passes a glance and is
wrong underneath. If what EDGAR publishes bundles a filer's metadata with part of
its filing list, that is the shape of the source and not a violation of the step
boundary — the criterion is that a rejected filer costs nothing after its
verdict.

What a retrieved history becomes to the next stage is still not settled, and is
not settled here either: that is the fetch → normalize contract, which needs its
own ADR and the sign-off `contracts/` carries. `scripts/gates.sh` is protected
and not this task's to touch.

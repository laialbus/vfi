The milestone names, among the golden fixtures, a company whose statements do
not fit the ordinary shape, and says why it matters in its own words: a bank
has no gross profit, and pretending otherwise would fabricate a number. The
vocabulary turned that into a closed set of five kinds and an `applies_to`
clause per concept, and `NotApplicable` is constructible only from a kind
together with a clause that excludes it. Both filers in the tree are
`operating` or argued to be, so on real data the third state is reachable only
where a test names a kind for itself. This is the recording of the filer that
will reach it honestly.

Which of the four kinds is the run's to choose, against the vocabulary's own
clause for it and the filer's own facts. What the acceptance fixes is the
absence the milestone names, stated over the registry as it stands:
`gross_profit` applies to `operating` alone, and the registry reaches it by
`GrossProfit` or by a difference from one of four cost-of-revenue elements, so
a filer of the right shape reports under none of them. Five concepts in the
vocabulary carry a clause narrower than all five kinds, and `gross_profit` is
the one every non-operating kind is excluded from; the others are named in the
header where the filer's statements show them.

The kind itself is not asserted here. M4-21 and M4-22 show why: a kind is read
off the balance sheet and the income statement against the clause, a fixture's
expected result is read off the ruleset, and the two are decided against
different evidence in tasks of their own. This follows M4-28 only because both
write the harness header, and M4-25 because a facts case at v2 holds the
submissions document too. Not in this diff: anything that reads the case.

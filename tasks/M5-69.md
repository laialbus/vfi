GOALS.md's fifth M5 criterion opens its list of dividend metrics with "yield
and relative yield". The yield has its record, `docs/adr/dividend-yield.md`,
Accepted on main since #427, which leaves relative yield "to a record of its
own" and says under Consequences "Relative yield's record has a fixed yield
to stand on." The provider record already fixes which closes it reads and
whose reading it is, and the analyze record keeps a witness of `no_price`
for it alone. Its form, the years it reads, its conditions, its name and
its source have no record.

Why a record, and not the metric's task. Its name crosses `analyze-store` v1
as characters; how many past years are read is a free parameter or a
decision that there is none; whether a past year's yield at its own close
is a stored metric or a term is the question the yield record leaves open
by name; and what the past yields are reduced to is a reading a worker must
not guess.

Why now. Earlier plans held it for two reasons: the yield it stands on was
not fixed, and no open text stated it. The first is closed. For the second,
the newsletter the method comes from publishes the notion on its own pages,
which opened whole in this run. They state the notion and no formula, so
the record's form is its own decision on an open primary, as price/free
cash flow's form was the market capitalisation record's own. Weiss and Lowe
stays closed and is named as the provider record names it, and no more.

It depends on nothing because every record it reads is accepted and on main,
and a record does not wait on the yield's code. It owns one new file, so it
runs beside M5-65 and M5-68 on `crates/analyze/`. The metric is queued under
the record's acceptance, not here.

If the record finds the form cannot be stated without an operation outside
the arithmetic record's table, a new reason, a change to a frozen contract's
bytes or a `unit` the entry shape does not carry, it says so by name as the
acceptance above provides. If it cannot be written at all without an
accepted record changing, that is an escalation.

  - Its home is recorded in `docs/layout.md` in the same diff. Placement is a

  - The declaration it sends arrives as an argument. The engine holds no
    per-user state and reads no environment, config file, or constant for one.
  - At least one case under `fixtures/fetch/` is recorded by it against live
    EDGAR and committed. Recording is a step a person takes and never a step a
    test takes, exactly as the harness header already says.
  - `tests/golden.rs`'s header names this entry point as the way to record a
    case, in place of the curl line it carries now. One way to record a
    fixture, written down in the one place that says how.
  - Where a refreshed body changes what its case expects, the `expected` beside
    it is rewritten from the recording rather than by running the stage. Two
    readings agreeing is the whole of what a golden case checks, and an
    expected result the subject generated proves only that the subject agrees
    with itself.
  - Every request it sends leaves through M3-01's chokepoint, at M3-02's pace,
    and reaches only a listed host. It is a caller of the stage like any other
    and gets no shorter path because it is a tool.
  - The session entry says which case was recorded and what EDGAR answered, so
    the one run that reached the source is on record.
  - Nothing proved gets narrower: the fixtures gate passes over every case, the
    egress gate still holds, and every existing proof still passes. Nothing
    added here makes a test reach the network.

M3-09 builds a transport nobody has watched fetch anything. M0 already settled
what that is worth: "A block nobody has watched refuse something is
indistinguishable from no block." The same holds in the other direction, and
this task is the watching — one recording, taken from EDGAR through the
engine's own path, committed as data.

It is worth more than a demonstration. Every fixture in the tree was recorded
by curl, so what they pin is what curl received. What the engine receives is
whatever its own transport does with the response — the decompression, the
headers, the redirect it did or did not follow — and until one case has been
recorded through that path, the fixtures pin a client the engine is not. Moving
the recording instruction onto the engine closes that gap and keeps it closed.

Pick the case whose expected result is cheapest to re-derive by hand. Refreshing
a thousand-filing history is not what this task is for, and a case whose
`expected` is a single stated outcome costs a line.

Two things this may turn up, and both are yours to fix here rather than defer:
a transport that gets bytes curl did not, and a fixture whose recording no
longer matches what EDGAR publishes. That is why this owns `crates/fetch/`
alongside M3-09.

GOALS.md's fourth M5 criterion names returns on capital, and
`docs/adr/canonical-concepts-open-questions.md` admits `preferred_equity`
and `preferred_dividends` for the common-shareholder forms.
`docs/adr/common-shareholder-forms.md`, accepted on 2026-10-14, is the
record that forms them: common equity and income available to common, each
once in one module, the common dividend total left open, the naming rule,
and `zero_common_equity`. Its "Where a flow meets a balance" leaves the
instant to `docs/adr/which-other-period-a-metric-reads.md`, accepted the
same day, which names return on common equity as averaged. With both
accepted, this metric is the first that reads the module, and the module is
built here so that no later metric forms a term itself.

Why this metric first. It is the one common form that reads both terms,
so building it builds the whole module; it reads no price and needs no
constant or setting; and `return_on_equity` stands beside it from M5-30, so
the proof the record lists, the total and common forms at one row, can be
shown in one history.

It depends on M5-30 because it reads the seeks and the averaged read M5-29
builds and stands beside the total form M5-30 builds, and because all three
own `crates/analyze/`.

If the metric cannot be built without a seventh reason, a change to a
contract, to `method.rs`, to `settings/` or to the entry shape M5-18 fixed,
or if the record's proof cannot be met under the averaged reading as the
acceptance states it, that is an escalation.

M4 turns filings into comparable numbers, and normalize cannot begin until it
knows what it is given. What fetch produces today is the history index — what a
filer filed, when, and where its documents are — and `crates/fetch/src/filing.rs`
says outright that the columns beyond that "arrive when a stage has a use for
one, and what the next stage is owed is the fetch → normalize contract, which is
not settled here." This is that.

Two questions are settled together because they are one question. Which document
the facts come out of decides what fetch retrieves, and what fetch retrieves
bounds what can cross. Answering only the second produces a contract with fields
nothing stands behind.

The weight is on provenance. M4 requires every resolved value to record its
source tag, its filing, and the rule that set it. The rule is normalize's own;
the first two are not — they exist only in what fetch hands over. A contract
that carries a number without them reads fine and fails much later, when the
fixtures are being written and there is nothing to write down.

A contract between stages is above a worker: anchor 3 fixes that boundaries are
typed and versioned, and AGENTS.md puts any change to one behind an ADR. So this
is a proposal, and the contract itself is built by the task that follows the
decision.

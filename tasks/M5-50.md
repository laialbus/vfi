GOALS.md's fourth M5 criterion names "financial health and bankruptcy
scoring, the established screens". `docs/adr/piotroski-signals-and-score.md`,
accepted on 2026-10-20, fixes the nine signals and the score to the last
step: each signal's form, benchmark and comparison, the instant each term
reads, the eight conditions and their order, the ten names, and how absence
composes over two years and three instants. It asks that the nine be formed
once, in `metrics/piotroski.rs`, and that each signal be stored as a metric
beside the score. Its proof section works every figure above by hand, and
the three signals here bring their share of it.

Why these three first, and alone. F_ROA, F_CFO and F_ACCRUAL read the
computing year and its opening instant, and nothing of the year before, so
they need no seek beyond the ones every averaged metric already makes; they
share one divisor and one condition; and they are the first metrics to read
an opening balance alone and the first to carry `Unit::Signals`. The module
they create is the one the six comparing signals and the score are added
to, so that each signal is formed once and the score calls what the signal
metrics call. Splitting the record across four tasks keeps each diff
reviewable; the record's one-module rule is what keeps the split from
duplicating a signal.

It depends on M5-49 because both own `crates/analyze/`, and because its new
test files pin the six-setting list that task leaves.

If a signal cannot be built without a seventh reason, a change to a
contract, to `method.rs`, to the entry shape M5-18 fixed, to `settings/` or
to anything M5-45 to M5-49 defined, if the opening balance cannot be read
alone through the accepted seek without changing another metric's reads, or
if the copy on reading forms a signal otherwise than the record, that is an
escalation.

GOALS.md's fourth M5 criterion ends with "discounted cash flow with margin
of safety", and its ninth says a metric with no price data "never shows a
wrong number". `docs/adr/what-analyze-takes-and-returns.md` (Accepted)
fixes the share-basis rule that keeps a price off a figure on another share
basis and asks that it be written once. `docs/adr/share-basis-rule-dates.md`,
accepted on 2026-10-26, settles the one question that stopped the first
task to write it: every comparison goes through the one ordering `date.rs`
holds, and a date the rule cannot read declines under one named condition.
`docs/adr/discounted-cash-flow-and-margin-of-safety.md` (Accepted) makes
the margin the first metric to read the crossing and leaves how it reaches
the crossing to the implementing task, inside three limits: read only
through the three `no_price` witnesses and the rule, no other metric's
reads or figures changing, and `Prices` to store unchanged.

Why the rule has a run of its own, before the margin. Every price-dependent
metric after the margin calls this function, so its proof is the share-basis
record's whole case table read against one function, which is a review of
its own; the margin's task then composes it with the value and brings the
same table through the hand-over. The reading of the close is put beside it
because the record lists the price read's reasons in one order, no crossing,
an absence, the rule, then the close's grammar, and this task takes that
listing as the order. If that reading is wrong, the one test that pins it,
`1e2` beside an absent split list, is the line to change.

It depends on M5-53 because both own `crates/analyze/`, and the Piotroski
chain M5-50 to M5-53 is in flight there.

If the rule cannot be written within the six reasons, needs a second
ordering or a comparison of characters, needs a change to a contract, to
`date.rs` beyond its documentation, to the entry shape M5-18 fixed, or to
anything M5-36 to M5-53 defined, or if a case in the record's table cannot
be given the outcome the record states, that is an escalation.

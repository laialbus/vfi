The registry answers which rules may reach a concept for a filer. It does not
answer which one wins when two of them match facts in one filing, and it says so
in its own words: entries for a concept are a set, their order means nothing, and
naming the cases it hands on is not deciding them. Until this record exists,
resolution cannot be written — the first agent to try would decide seven
methodology questions inside a function, one plausible line at a time, which is
the shape of mistake that passes a glance and corrupts results quietly.

Two things are readable now that were not when the earlier records deferred this.
The fetch surface is published and frozen, so what crosses into normalize is
fixed in bytes rather than argued. And the first company-facts fixture is merged
— every fact one filer reported, keyed by taxonomy, tag, unit, period and
accession, with no collision on that key — so whether a segment-dimensioned fact
reaches this stage at all is something to read rather than to assume. Read it.
The milestone asks for consolidated over segment, and a rule written against
facts that never arrive is a rule nobody can check.

The period half is where the two remaining records touch, and the touch is worth
drawing carefully. Which of a filing's durations answers a flow concept, and
whether an instant answers a balance, is this record's. Which filing a canonical
period is built from when a later one restates an earlier one is the alignment
ruleset's, and the milestone separates it by name. A record that quietly took
some of the second would leave the next one with a rule already half-written in a
file it was told not to read.

This depends on nothing in the queue beside it. It reads accepted records and
published surfaces, all of them on main, and it touches no crate, no manifest,
no gate and no data.

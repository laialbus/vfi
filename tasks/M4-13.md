`docs/adr/candidate-choice.md` is accepted, and it names this task in its own
Enforcement section: the two fields are "added by the task that writes the
fields", and the two gate checks over them come in the same diff. Both halves of
the registry are data on disk and the one interface over them is in, so the
fields have somewhere to land and something to carry them.

What is being decided here is nothing. The record fixed the two closed value
sets and the test that assigns the first, and it fixed why the ordering is an
intrinsic field rather than a rank: `reading` is a claim about one entry against
one published meaning, answerable with the rest of the file unread, so two runs
adding entries in parallel cannot make it inconsistent. Assigning it entry by
entry is the work. The seven cases the record walks through — `net_income`,
`revenue`, `gross_profit`, `income_tax_expense`, `earnings_per_share_diluted`,
`dividends_paid`, `capital_expenditure` — are worked examples of that test, not
seven separate answers, and an assignment that disagrees with one of them is
wrong about the test rather than about the case.

The record is equally clear about what no gate can check here: whether an
entry's `reading` is the right one is a judgement against a meaning clause
written in prose, and a stand-in wrongly marked `exact` silently outranks the
entry that was right. Only a fixture whose expected value disagrees will catch
it. That is the reason to make each assignment against the vocabulary's own
words rather than against what an element's name suggests.

Not in this diff: the procedure. The four steps that turn eligible rules into a
`Value` or an `Unknown` read `reading` at step two, and nothing reads it yet.
This lands the data the procedure will be written against, so the run that
writes it is arguing about one thing.

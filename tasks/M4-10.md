The accepted record puts two halves under `registry/`, and M4-09 lands the first.
This is the second: the per-company override the milestone's criterion names in
the same breath as the registry itself — "a versioned registry with per-company
overrides" — and the kind assertion, which is what a filer's `revenue` reading
depends on and what every concept's applicability is asked against.

Nothing here competes with the general mapping. `include` adds a rule for one
filer, `exclude` removes one by its id, and the two compose to one eligible set
however they are written, so there is no precedence contest to settle.
Assertion is the single exception the record makes total, and it is the one place
in normalize where a number arrives with no tag behind it — which is why it
carries a required `source`, the accession and the line it was read from. Nothing
can check that the citation is true. What the required field buys is that an
assertion with no stated ground cannot be written at all.

The refusals matter more than usual because what they catch is a defect that
would otherwise resolve to something. A rule both included and excluded, or two
assertions whose periods overlap, is ambiguous override data; the record is
explicit that neither is settled by picking one and neither becomes `Unknown`,
because `Unknown` means a filing was consulted and gave nothing, and hiding a
data defect behind it is exactly the plausible-looking wrong answer this
milestone exists to prevent.

The gate file is protected, granted to this task because this file lists it under
`owns`, and the merge needs the human-approved label that every protected-path
change needs. Exclusive for the same reason M4-09 is: it changes the gates.

What follows and is not in this diff: the one interface both halves are reached
through, and the record that decides which of several matching rules wins.

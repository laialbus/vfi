The fetch → normalize surface says what fetch hands normalize and where it reads
it: EDGAR's collation of the filer's own XBRL exhibits, one request per filer,
so a fact reaches normalize stamped with the filing it was reported in without
that filing being retrieved. Nothing retrieves it. This is the second of the
three gaps M4-03 left behind, and the mapping cannot start against a boundary
that carries nothing.

The fixture is not a nicety attached to the retrieval; it is the only thing that
can show the three properties hold. "Fetch filters nothing" and "nothing is
parsed" are claims about a document nobody has read yet, and a stage that
quietly dropped a taxonomy would pass every test written from the contract's
prose. So the fixture is recorded from a real filer through the stage's own
chokepoint — `crates/fetch/src/bin/record.rs` is what M3-10 built for exactly
this — and what comes back is what the expected result is written from.

The third property is where this task can go wrong quietly, and it is the one
place the answer is not this task's to give.
`docs/adr/fetch-normalize-contract.md` could not settle by reading whether the
retrieval publishes a dimensioned fact, so it made the question detectable
instead: a fact is identified by taxonomy, tag, unit, period and accession, and
two facts sharing those five with different values is a collision. If the
recorded document holds one, that is the signal that dimensions cross, and what
to do about it is a rule about choosing among candidates — a separate record,
and above this task. Escalate with the collision in hand. Do not pick a value,
and do not filter the loser away to make the property true.

Depends on M4-06 because the value it hands back is that task's type, and
because a stage may not depend on `vfi-contracts` until the dependency gate
permits the edge.

What follows this task and is not in it: the funnel's fourth step, which turns
this retrieval into a verdict per filer the way the third step did; and the rule
for choosing among several candidates in one filing, which is shaped by what
this fixture turns out to hold.

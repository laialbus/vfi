The accepted alignment record sets one condition for admitting the entry that
answers the period of the filing it was reported in: "a filing answers a period
through an entry that answers the period of the filing it was reported in only
when that filing's own period of report ends on the period asked for." At v1 the
condition could not be stated, so M4-19 refused the entry on the door — every
filing asked with `OnlyThePeriodAskedFor` — and measured what admitting it
unchecked would cost: `shares_outstanding` at 2023-12-31 handed a count from a
cover page nineteen months later. M4-25 publishes the field the condition names.
This is the condition, stated.

What changes is small and inside one module: which `Admits` a filing is asked
with is no longer a constant but a reading of that filing's own report period
end against the instant asked for. Nothing in `answering` or `settling` moves —
the entry's fact is found and ranked exactly as those modules already do — and
the only thing a filing can newly gain is a stand-in candidate that step 2 drops
wherever the filer tagged a period-end count. That is the reason the accepted
candidate-choice record priced the entry as a stand-in and the registry marks it
one: a count at the cover date is the count at the period end only for a filer
that issued nothing in between. Where the filer tagged nothing else, the cover
count is a value naming its own date and its filing, which is an answer with its
provenance and not a number nobody stated.

The strict half of "ends on the period asked for" is taken deliberately, and the
argument is M4-17's. A balance is answered at the instant it is asked at and not
over a duration; the alignment ruleset makes every date a fact carries a period
of its own, so nothing is unreachable under that reading, and the loose one
would key one figure under two periods, one of which the figure does not
describe. A run that finds the fixture contradicts this stops.

Not in this diff: Rule 3, which is stated two ways in the accepted record and
waits on the record superseding it; Rule 1, which asks Rule 3's answer; and
what the stage emits once wired. Nothing here orders two filings, parses
`filed`, or reads a form.

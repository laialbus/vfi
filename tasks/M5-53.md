GOALS.md's fourth M5 criterion names "financial health and bankruptcy
scoring, the established screens". `docs/adr/piotroski-signals-and-score.md`,
accepted on 2026-10-20, fixes the score as the count of the nine signals,
its eighteen reads and their order, the one read of `total_assets` at Y's
opening, how its reasons are its signals' reasons given once, the order of
its eight conditions, and why a filer whose shape lacks a signal's concept
has no score. `bare-literal-lint.md` places the count of nine in
`constants/` with the signals as `[bool; PIOTROSKI_SIGNALS]`. The record's
proof section works every score above by hand, and this task brings them.

Why the score last. It is built on the nine functions the three tasks
before it form, and the record requires that it call them rather than form a
signal itself; so it can be written only once all nine exist, and it is the
first metric to carry a constant of the method since M5-36's calendar
numbers. Its tests are the record's whole table read through one name,
which is a review of its own.

It depends on M5-52 because it calls the functions that task and the two
before it formed, and because both own `crates/analyze/`.

If the score cannot be built without a seventh reason, a change to a
contract, to `method.rs`, to the entry shape M5-18 fixed, to `settings/`, or
to anything M5-45 to M5-52 defined, if a shared read cannot be made once
without changing a signal metric's reads, or if the copy on reading forms the
score otherwise than the record, that is an escalation.

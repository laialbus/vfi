This is the procedure `docs/adr/candidate-choice.md` decides, and every question
it would otherwise have to answer for itself has been answered by a record or
landed by a task ahead of it. Applicability comes from the vocabulary and is
already asked. The assertion is total by the registry record. The eligible rules
arrive through the one interface, already reduced by kind scope and by the
filer's own `include` and `exclude`, each carrying the `reading` that step two
needs. Which facts answer the period is the answer next door. What is left is the
four steps and what each outcome carries — which is why this task can be about
one thing.

The record is emphatic about the shape of the failure it is avoiding, and the
acceptance above is strict for that reason alone. Two candidates that agree are
still `Unknown`, because a `Value` records "the rule that set it", singular, and
a rule picked among equals is a choice nothing could replay. A partial
composition is not a candidate. A collision on the five-field key is not resolved
by preferring the plausible number, because nothing crossing the boundary says
which number is which. Every one of those costs a metric that M5 will show as
absent with a reason, and every one is recoverable by a per-filer `exclude` or
`assert` — a file with a rule id behind it, which is the difference between a
choice someone signed and a choice a function made quietly.

What no gate can check here is worth reading before starting rather than after.
The record names it: whether an entry's `reading` is the right one is a judgement
against prose, and a stand-in wrongly marked `exact` silently outranks the entry
that was right. This task does not revisit those assignments — they are M4-13's,
made per entry against the vocabulary's own words — but the two fixture contests
it exercises are the first place a wrong one would show, and an outcome that
surprises the run is a reason to stop and escalate rather than to adjust a step.

Not in this diff, and the reason the batch ends here: nothing runs over a filer's
history. Which canonical periods a filer has, which filing answers each, and what
a restatement or an amendment does to one already resolved are
`docs/adr/period-alignment.md`, proposed by M4-14 and not accepted. Until it is,
the period and the filing are the caller's to name — which is exactly the seam the
accepted record draws — and the golden fixtures the milestone names have no rule
to be chosen against.

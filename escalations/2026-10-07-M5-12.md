# M5-12 — the accepted record says three reasons both carry and do not carry their witness's fields

- Date: 2026-10-07
- Branch: M5-12
- Commit: 9948d77

**Doing.** Writing `contracts/analyze-store/v1.toml`'s `[[reason]]` entries.
Each entry needs a `carries` list, the way `fetch-analyze` v1 has one. Nothing
is committed except this file.

**What stopped it.** The acceptance says each reason "carries what the record
says it carries and nothing the witnessing state already holds". For three
reasons, `docs/adr/what-analyze-takes-and-returns.md` makes those two
requirements disagree:

- `no_price.answered_absent` (line 285): "carries the date asked and the
  `price-provider.md` reason". Its witness is the crossing's
  `Answer::Absence`, which already holds `date_asked` and `reason`.
- `basis_unproven.splits_absent` (line 294): "carries the `price-provider.md`
  reason". Its witness is `Splits::Absence`, which already holds `reason`.
- `basis_unproven.split_within` (line 299): "names the split's date and factor
  as published". Its witness is a `SplitFactor` in the list, which already
  holds `date` and `factor`.

Line 307 states the general rule: "A reason names the input's state and does
not copy what that state carries … so each stays in one copy." The two history
reasons follow it, since each names a concept and a period and copies neither
the kind nor the attempts. The three reasons above break it. No reading
satisfies both lines, and whichever one I pick is frozen into the reason set,
which the record lists as expensive to reverse.

**What would let it proceed.** A ruling, as an edit to the task's acceptance or
a note on the record, that picks one of these:

1. **The explicit lists win.** The three reasons carry what lines 285, 294 and
   299 say. Line 307's rule then covers the history's states only, and the
   acceptance's "nothing the witnessing state already holds" is reworded to
   match.
2. **The rule at line 307 wins.** `answered_absent` carries the date asked
   only, `splits_absent` carries nothing, and `split_within` names the split
   by its date without its factor. Lines 285, 294 and 299 then say more than
   the contract does. One caveat: naming a split by its date assumes the list
   holds at most one split per date, and `fetch-analyze` v1 does not state
   that.

The rest of the surface has no conflict, so once the ruling lands the task can
run unchanged.

For the decider. Three judgements worth a look.

A `Value` here is not `Resolution::Value`. That shape asks for a source tag, a
filing and a rule, and only a value a rule read out of facts has all three: an
asserted value has no tag, and a value the silence reading supplies has none of
the three. So the state carries what set it — a rule over named facts, an
assertion, or the published silence reading — rather than an empty string where a
tag would go. Crossing that into the published shape belongs to the task that
wires the stage; nothing crosses yet.

A candidate is a rule together with the facts it read, so one rule yields two
candidates where two facts collide on the five fields that identify one, or where
one entry answers under two currency keys. Both then reach step 4 undecided,
which is how the collision is carried without a seventh decline reason. Step 3's
own words — "a candidate whose facts are strictly contained in another
survivor's facts" — are the argument.

A conditional silence reading refuses to be asked twice on one walk. The
condition names one concept; that concept's rules could name a third by a
`difference` and walk back, and the registry gate is over difference edges, not
over this one. The second condition returns "not silent", which costs the zero
and never the other direction.

Also: `sum` and `difference` compose their figures digit by digit, at the scale
of whichever operand publishes the most places. No float, and no cap on the
digits — a cap would be a number with no source.
